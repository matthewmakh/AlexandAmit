02 — Executive summary

This is not a generic template. It is a well-written site with four specific defects.

The copywriting here is better than almost every wedding site I have read. "Nothing is compulsory — come to as much or as little as you like." "The part we have been rehearsing in the kitchen." "The aqueduct at sunset — go late." That voice is warm, formal, specific, and nothing like a template. It is the site's greatest asset and I would not change a word of it.

The structure beneath it is also sound. The wedding day already runs hour by hour from 17:00 to midnight. The travel page is organised the way a traveller thinks — arriving, transportation, good to know. The metadata is properly done, page by page.

So what follows is not a redesign. It is a repair, a subtraction, and a promotion: fix what is visibly broken, delete what is said three times, and give the dress code and the shuttles the prominence guests will actually go looking for.

One structural recommendation runs through everything: nine top-level pages is too many for a wedding. Collapsing to five destinations is the largest single clarity gain available, and it costs no content — every word survives, in a more findable place.

Defect 01
The monogram renders as "A AAA"

On every page, several times per page — including as the clickable site logo. Screen readers announce your logo as "A AAA".

Defect 02
The same navigation, three times

Nine links in the header, the same nine in the footer, and the same nine again as "The guide" 01–09 on the homepage.

Defect 03
A splash gate where RSVP should be

The hero's only action is "Enter Website", which scrolls one section down. The most important button on the site is not in the first screen.

Defect 04
"You are invited", twice in a row

The homepage carries the same heading and the same invitation wording in two consecutive sections, plus a live countdown between them.

03 — What should stay

Preserve, untouched.

All copy, verbatim

Every line on the site, in both the formal register ("request the pleasure of your company … at six o'clock in the evening") and the warm register ("An afternoon to do very little"). This is the voice. It moves, it does not get rewritten.

The wedding-day timeline

17:00 reception, 18:00 ceremony, 18:45 cocktails, 19:45 dinner, 21:00 dancing, 00:00 late night. Correct structure, correct level of detail. Keep as is and give it more visual weight.

Travel page architecture

Arriving / Transportation / Good to know is exactly how a guest reasons about a trip. TLV 45 min, Haifa 40 min, Binyamina 10 min by taxi — concrete and useful. Keep the whole structure.

"Our Caesarea"

Named restaurants, named beaches, real opinions. The most luxurious thing on the site, because it could not have been generated. Keep every entry.

RSVP field set & logic

Name, email, accept/decline, party size, meal preference, note. The right questions in the right order. I will not touch the form logic, the submission, or where replies land.

Warm ivory ground

Your theme colour is already #F7F5F0. The palette instinct is right — it needs codifying into a token set, not replacing.

Per-page metadata

Distinct titles, descriptions, og:type article/website, Twitter cards. Better than most professional builds. Leave it alone.

Descriptive alt text

"Riding horses through the shallows at sunset", "Dan Caesarea gardens at dusk". Already written, already good. Keep and extend to any new image.

Working links

The Dan Hotels booking URL, the Google Maps directions query, and the tel: link on Eden's number all resolve. Carried over character for character.

04 — High-impact improvements

Nine changes, in priority order.

Each carries its own risk level and approval line, so you can approve them individually rather than as a block.

H1Repair the monogram
Both Low risk Approval required
Current

The AA monogram outputs the text "A AAA" — as the linked logo in the header, in the footer, and as a section ornament. It appears roughly six times on the homepage alone.

Problem

It reads as a rendering fault on the most formal element of the site, and a screen reader announces the home link as "A AAA". Stray glyphs on a wedding invitation undo the impression everything else is working for.

Proposed

Your own calligraphic A&A artwork — the overlapping letterforms and long curved flourish, cut to a transparent PNG at true aspect ratio — used as one image asset across the site rather than typed text, wrapped in an aria-label="Amit and Alex — home". A charcoal recolour (identical artwork, same alpha, no shape changes) is used over the pale hero sky where the original gold tested too low-contrast; the gold original is kept for ivory and darker sections further down the site.

Improvement

Every guest, every page. Removes the one defect that currently makes the site look unfinished, and fixes the logo for assistive technology at the same time.

Functionality impact — none. Presentational component only; the home link's destination is unchanged.
H2Nine pages become five
Both Medium risk Approval required
Current

Home, Our Story, Wedding Weekend, Travel, Hotels, Registry, Gallery, FAQ, Contact — nine header links, the same nine repeated in the footer, and the same nine a third time as the "The guide" 01–09 index on the homepage.

Problem

Nine choices is a directory, not a wedding. On a phone the menu becomes a scrolling list; the guide section repeats it a third time without adding information. Travel and Hotels are one decision for a guest, split across two pages.

Proposed

The Wedding · The Weekend · Travel & Stay · Questions · Gallery, plus RSVP as a button and EN/עב always visible. Travel and Hotels merge into one page with anchors. Registry, FAQ, and Contact become sections of Questions. Our Story moves under The Wedding. Delete "The guide" entirely. Old URLs redirect — no link a guest has already been sent should break.

Improvement

A guest planning the trip reads flights, hotel, and shuttle in one scroll instead of jumping pages. The mobile menu fits one screen with no scrolling. Nothing is removed — every word keeps a home.

Functionality impact — routing. Old paths must be redirected, and the language switcher must be verified on the merged pages. This is the one item where I would want to see the repo before touching anything.
H3Remove the "Enter Website" gate
Both Low risk Approval required
Current

The hero shows the names, the date, "Kochav Hayam · Caesarea, Israel", and a single button: "Enter Website", which jumps to #invitation. RSVP is in the header bar only.

Problem

The most prominent action on the site accomplishes nothing but a scroll, and "Enter Website" is the language of a 2004 splash page — the one wrong note in otherwise beautiful copy. Meanwhile the reply deadline of 15 September never appears in the first screen.

Proposed

Replace it with a single RSVP pill in the header — present on every screen. The desktop hero itself now carries only the names, date, and venue, so the first screen reads as an invitation rather than a form; the reply deadline moves to the second RSVP moment after Wedding Details, once a guest has read the key facts.

Improvement

Names, date, venue, city, RSVP, and the deadline all in the first screen on the smallest phone. A guest in a hurry never scrolls or opens a menu.

Functionality impact — none. Adds a link to the existing /rsvp route; the form itself is untouched.
H4Crop the hero for a portrait photograph
Both Low risk Approval required
Current

One image — hero-horses.png — heads every page on the site and is also the social share card. It is a tall portrait frame: pale sky across the top half, the two of you on horseback in the lower third, teal water, a boat between you, and a third horse cropped at the right edge.

Problem

A portrait image forced into a wide desktop hero must crop away either the sky or the two of you. And because you sit in the lower third, bottom-anchored hero text lands directly on top of you. The same photograph on all nine pages also spends its impact — by the third page it is wallpaper. It is a PNG, which is the wrong format for a photograph.

Proposed

Two different treatments rather than one resize. Mobile: near-full portrait frame with the names set into the empty sky above you in ink — no dark scrim needed, because the sky is already pale. Desktop: full-bleed to the very top of the page, an outpainted wide crop with the header overlaid transparently on the photo and the same type-in-the-sky treatment, just wider. Different photograph per page; WebP with a PNG fallback.

Improvement

Your faces are never covered by type or cut by a crop, at any width. Contrast stops depending on an overlay. And the photograph keeps its power because it is not repeated nine times.

Functionality impact — none, but it needs your photo library. I have one image; per-page heroes need more. Format conversion is a build change, not a content change.
H5Give the dress code its own home
Both Low risk Approval required
Current

"Black tie" appears three times as a bare label — in Wedding details, in Travel's Good to know, and on the invitation. The genuinely useful guidance lives inside a collapsed FAQ answer: floor-length gowns, tuxedos or dark evening suits; outdoors on stone and grass; block heels are the kinder choice; dinner indoors.

Problem

Dress code is one of the top three things guests search a wedding site for, especially guests flying in and packing a case. Right now the answer requires finding the FAQ page and expanding the first accordion. Two of the three labels say "Black tie" and nothing more, which for a garden-and-stone venue is not enough to pack by.

Proposed

A short dress section on The Wedding page, using your existing FAQ wording verbatim, plus the per-event codes you already have — smart casual for Shabbat dinner, black tie for Sunday. No lookbook, no shopping grid, no mood board. Type and one photograph.

Improvement

Guests packing from abroad get the answer without hunting, and the practical detail about stone and grass reaches the people it protects.

Functionality impact — none. Existing copy, repositioned. The FAQ entry stays where it is.
H6Consolidate transportation — and flag what is missing
Both Low risk Approval required
Current

Shuttle information is spread across three pages: Travel says shuttles run between Dan Caesarea and the venue on the wedding day; Hotels repeats it as a bullet; the Weekend page says buses to the Namos party leave from Dan Caesarea and return after. The FAQ has a collapsed "Is transportation provided?" entry.

Problem

No departure time appears anywhere, for any transfer. That is the single most-asked question of a destination wedding, and the absence reads as an omission rather than as "not yet decided". Guests deciding whether to hire a car cannot decide.

Proposed

One Transportation block on Travel & Stay listing every transfer you have already confirmed — airport, Namos buses, wedding-day shuttle, parking — each with an explicit "Departure times to be confirmed" marker where the time is unknown. I will not invent a single time.

Improvement

Guests see that transport exists and is handled, know what is still being finalised, and stop phoning Eden to ask. One place to update later instead of three.

Functionality impact — none. Needs your confirmed times when you have them; until then the placeholders stay visible and clearly marked.
H7Make the room block bookable
Both Medium risk Approval required
Current

Dan Caesarea is the host hotel: five minutes from the venue, block rates until 1 September, shuttle on the wedding day. "Reserve a room" opens a Dan Hotels search page, and the instruction is to mention the Shushan–Farkas wedding at checkout. The three alternatives — Ramada Netanya, Elma Zichron Ya'akov, local apartments — have no links at all.

Problem

A self-service booking engine has no checkout field for "mention the wedding", so guests will either pay rack rate or call. The 1 September deadline is a quiet bullet, not a warning — and it falls two weeks before the RSVP deadline, which nothing on the site points out. No rate figure is shown, so guests cannot budget.

Proposed

Restructure as a scannable key–value card — nights, rate, book-by, code, shuttle, cancellation — with the deadline in oxblood, the strongest colour on the site. Add whatever booking mechanism Dan actually gave you: a group code, a dedicated link, or a phone number. Link the three alternatives to their own sites.

Improvement

Guests actually get the rate you negotiated, and book before 1 September rather than after. Two hotels can be compared by reading down one column.

Functionality impact — external links. Flagged high for approval: I will not alter, shorten, or replace the Dan Hotels URL, and I need you to tell me the real group-rate mechanism rather than guessing. Rates and policies will not be invented.
H8Cut the duplicate invitation and the countdown
Both Low risk Approval required
Current

The homepage runs: "You are invited" with the invitation in prose, then a live countdown in days / hours / minutes / seconds, then "Together with their families — You are invited" again with the same invitation set as a formal letterpress card.

Problem

The same words twice reads as a build mistake, not as emphasis. The countdown ticks every second — the only continuously moving element on an otherwise still, formal site — and renders as em dashes until the script runs. It is the one element here that belongs on a party invitation rather than a black-tie one.

Proposed

Keep the letterpress invitation card — it is the more beautiful of the two — and delete the prose duplicate. Remove the countdown; if you want the sense of time approaching, "Kindly reply by 15 September" carries it with more restraint. Both are your call: the countdown is the one item where I am overruling something you may love.

Improvement

Roughly a screen and a half less scrolling on mobile to reach the weekend, one clear invitation moment instead of two competing ones, and no ticking.

Functionality impact — removes a timer component. Content deletion, so it needs explicit approval; I will not remove either section without it.
H9Audit the Hebrew version
Both Unknown risk Blocked
Current

An EN / עברית switcher is present on every page, twice in the markup — once for the desktop bar and once for the mobile bar. I could not read the Hebrew pages themselves.

Problem

Half your guests are Hebrew speakers, and this is the half of the site I know nothing about. Two parallel nav implementations also mean any label change has to be made twice or the languages drift apart.

Proposed

Before any Hebrew work: check untranslated strings, RTL mirroring, whether dates and phone numbers reverse, whether the Hebrew face is a real serif or a sans fallback, and whether switching language holds your place on the page. Section 08 sets out the typographic rules I would apply.

Improvement

Israeli guests get a site that was designed rather than translated — the difference is immediately obvious to a native reader.

Functionality impact — potentially the language switcher itself, which is why I will not touch it before reading the code. Blocked pending repo access or a walkthrough.
05 — Medium impact & minor polish

Medium

FAQ answers depend on JavaScript

Only the dress-code answer is present in the delivered markup; the other ten arrive empty. Render all answers in the markup and collapse them with CSS, so a slow connection still shows content.

No email address anywhere

The RSVP form asks guests for an email, but Contact offers only Eden's phone number. Some guests — particularly older ones abroad — will want to write rather than call an international number. Your decision; I will not invent an address.

RSVP confirmation state unverified

I cannot see what happens after "Send reply". Guests must get an unmistakable confirmation, and a clear error if it fails. Needs testing against the real integration — no changes to submission logic.

Reply deadline missing from the footer

The footer carries nine links but not the one date that has a deadline. Add "Kindly reply by 15 September 2026" and Eden's number beside the RSVP link.

"Play music" is buried in the footer

Either move it somewhere discoverable or remove it. If it ever plays without a tap, that needs fixing first — audio on load is the fastest way to make a guest close the tab.

Hero PNG

A photograph delivered as PNG is several times larger than it needs to be, on the image every page loads first. Convert to WebP, add explicit dimensions to prevent layout shift, and lazy-load everything below the first screen.

The Lovable badge is on the published site

An "Edit with" link sits in the corner of every page. Guests should not see the tooling. Removable in project settings.

Minor

"Amit&Alex" in the hero

Set without spaces in the hero, but as "Amit & Alex" everywhere else. Use a hair space either side of the ampersand and set it in the serif italic — a small detail that reads as care.

"11 . 10 . 2026"

Spaced periods in the footer read as three separate numbers. Either "11.10.2026" with letter-spacing, or spell the date. Also ambiguous to American guests, for whom 11.10 reads as November.

Stray bullet in "Number attending"

A list marker renders beside the party-size options. Cosmetic, on the most important form on the site.

Block-rate deadline needs weight

"Block rates until 1 September" sits as the second of three bullets. It is a financial deadline; it should look like one.

One accent, because of the water

The hero's turquoise is the most saturated colour in the project. Gold ornament placed near it will fight it. Let the photograph carry the colour and keep the page ink-on-ivory — gold only as hairlines and the monogram dot.

Consistent separators

The site mixes ·, —, and commas for the same job. One rule: middle dot between facts, em dash only inside sentences.

01 · The sky is the text box

Your photograph has an enormous pale sky above you and dark water below. Setting the names into that sky in ink needs no scrim at all — better contrast than white-on-photo, and the two of you are never covered.

02 · Desktop mirrors mobile

Same composition at both widths: full-bleed photograph, type in the sky, couple anchored low. Reaching a wide frame without stretching or cropping them out required outpainting — sky extended upward, water and horizon extended sideways, the couple and horses left untouched. The header then overlays that photograph transparently, so the page feels full-bleed from the very top.

Generate sky and water only. No retouching of faces, bodies, clothing, horses, boat, shoreline, lighting, or waterline. Keep the portrait original as the mobile asset — two files, art-directed separately.

03 · RSVP below the image on mobile

A solid ink button on ivory, at the bottom of the first screen where a thumb rests — rather than a translucent button floating over water. The deadline sits directly beneath it.

04 · Crop the third horse

A horse enters at the right edge of the frame. On the mobile crop it reads as an accident; the focal point should sit slightly left so the composition ends on Alex.

05 · One RSVP, then a second later

The hero now carries a single RSVP — the pill in the header — so the first screen reads as pure invitation: names, date, venue, nothing else. Text sits lower in the sky, closer to the water.
